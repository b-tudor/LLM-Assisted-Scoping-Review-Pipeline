1. Introduction 
Musculoskeletal  disorders  (MSDs)  are  prevalent  worldwide  and 
affect  1.7  billion  people  (Safiri  et  al.,  2021;  WHO,  2022).  MSDs  are 
caused by trauma, genetics, or repeated loading of the musculoskeletal 
system leading to muscle fatigue, and eventually overuse muscle injuries 
in the long term (Dugan and Frontera, 2000; Kumar, 2001; Pope, 1989; 
Rashedi  and  Nussbaum,  2015).  For  people  rehabilitating  from  MSDs, 
muscle fatigue influences their daily life activities including movement 
and balance (Jubany et al., 2017; Lin et al., 2009). 
Modeling  incidence  and  progression  of  fatigue  is  useful  in  under-
standing  muscle  mechanics  during  daily  life  to  avoid  risk  of  injury 
(Gallagher et al., 2017; Wan et al., 2017). Biologically realistic muscu-
loskeletal models are needed to understand muscle injury progression 
considering  the  dynamic  nature  of  the  muscle  and  the  underlying 
changes due to fatigue (Forde et al., 2002).
